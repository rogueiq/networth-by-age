What counts in net worth

The Fed defines net worth as “the difference between families’ gross assets and their liabilities” (2025 SCF report, Table 2 note).

  • Financial assets: transaction accounts (checking, savings, money market), certificates of deposit, savings bonds, other bonds, stocks, pooled investment funds, retirement accounts (IRAs, Keogh accounts and employer accounts such as 401(k)s and 403(b)s), cash value life insurance, other managed assets, and other financial assets.
  • Nonfinancial assets: vehicles, the primary residence, other residential property, equity in nonresidential property, business equity, and other items such as precious metals, art and collections.
  • Minus debts: mortgages and home equity lines of credit secured by the primary residence, other lines of credit, installment loans (education, vehicle and other), credit card balances, and other debt. Debt on businesses and nonresidential real estate is netted against those assets.

Social Security and employer defined-benefit pensions are left out, because their future income can’t easily be turned into a current value (2025 SCF report, Appendix A).

The unit is the family (close to a household), grouped by the age of the family’s reference person. The figures describe families, not individuals.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF report, Appendix A.

Change from 2022 to 2025

Families under 35 had a median net worth of $33,000 in 2025, down 23% from 2022 in inflation-adjusted terms (Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF).

Families 75 and older had a median net worth of $504,900 in 2025, up 37% from 2022 (Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF).

Methodology

  • Survey: Federal Reserve Board, 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, conducted every three years. Report released Oct 9, 2026.
  • Sample: 4,367 families interviewed in 2025 (4,602 in 2022). The public Summary Extract has 4,360 families.
  • Weights: every figure uses the extract’s survey weights (WGT).
  • Missing answers: the Fed fills in missing answers with multiple imputation, so the extract holds five versions (implicates) of each family. RogueIQ pools all five, weighted, which is the same approach that reproduces the Fed’s public-data tables.
  • Unit: families, grouped by the age of the reference person.
  • Dollars: 2025 dollars. The Fed converts 2022 values with the current-methods CPI.
  • Exclusions: people on the Forbes 400 list are excluded from the sample.
  • Percentiles: a percentile is the first net worth value where the cumulative weight reaches that share, rounded to the nearest $10.
  • Limits: the survey is a snapshot of balance sheets at the time of the interview (mostly April–December 2025, with about one-seventh of interviews in January–May 2026). The tails are noisy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the median net worth by age in 2025?

Median family net worth in 2025 was $33,000 under 35; $155,600 at 35–44; $253,700 at 45–54; $411,900 at 55–64; $431,300 at 65–74; $504,900 at 75 and older.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF, report Table 2 (released Oct 9, 2026). 2025 dollars.

What is the average net worth by age?

Mean (average) family net worth ranged from $135,400 under 35 to $1,962,200 at 75 and older in 2025. Means sit far above medians because a small number of very wealthy families pull the average up.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF, report Table 2 (released Oct 9, 2026). 2025 dollars.

What net worth puts you in the top 1% for your age?

RogueIQ’s calculation puts the 99th percentile at about $1.59M for families under 35, $8.35M at 35–44 and $14.05M at 45–54. These estimates rest on few survey families, so treat them as rough.

Source: computed by RogueIQ from the Federal Reserve’s 2025 SCF Summary Extract, survey-weighted. 2025 dollars.

What net worth is top 10% for my age?

The 90th percentile ranges from $351,200 for families under 35 to $4,188,260 for families 75 and older, by RogueIQ’s calculation.

Source: computed by RogueIQ from the Federal Reserve’s 2025 SCF Summary Extract, survey-weighted. 2025 dollars.

Is my net worth good for my age?

The survey can only show where a number ranks, and it can’t judge anyone’s finances. For example, $150,000 at age 35 is about the 49th percentile of families aged 35–44. Educational, not advice.

Source: RogueIQ net worth API (computed from the 2025 SCF Summary Extract).

Did net worth go up from 2022 to 2025?

Median family net worth rose 2% to $215,900 and the mean rose 7% to $1,241,500, in 2025 dollars. The under-35 median fell 23% while the 75-and-older median rose 37%.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF, report Table 2 (released Oct 9, 2026). 2025 dollars.

Is net worth measured by household or by individual?

By family. The SCF’s “family” is close to the Census Bureau’s “household,” and families are grouped by the age of a designated reference person.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF report, Appendix A.

When will the next update come out?

The SCF is conducted every three years, so the next survey would be the 2028 SCF. If the usual schedule holds, its results would come out around 2029.

Source: Federal Reserve, 2025 SCF report (triennial survey). Timing is an expectation.
